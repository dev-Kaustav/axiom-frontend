01 / THE BLIND SPOT

More positions.
Not necessarily
more diversification.

A list of tickers tells you what you own. It does not tell you how those positions behave together.

A meeting decision, a year-end level, and an “any time this year” contract can all resolve off the same rate path. Their prices sit in three separate markets. Your exposure does not.

Rook brings the underlying outcomes into view.

01

Hidden concentration

Several positions can win or lose on the same underlying event. A longer holdings list can still be one large directional bet.

02

Imperfect hedges

Similar headlines can carry different deadlines, thresholds and resolution rules. The apparent offset may not hold where it matters.

03

Disconnected decisions

A single trade moves more than one line in the book. See how it changes the whole position before you commit to it.

03 / A FEW GOOD QUESTIONS

Clarity starts
right here.

What is Rook?

A research workstation for prediction-market portfolios. It connects contract settlement rules to portfolio exposure, so you can explore outcomes, inspect computed relationships, and simulate changes to a book.

Who is it built for?

Traders and research teams running multiple prediction-market positions — especially where contracts share an underlying driver but differ in timing, threshold or settlement condition.

What can I try today?

The interactive demo carries a fictional portfolio over real Polymarket contracts on US rates. Explore exposure, scenarios, relationships, proposed trades and source evidence — no sign-up.

Is the data live? Can I place trades?

No. The demo runs on snapshot data over a fictional book. It is a research and simulation environment; it does not connect to your account and does not execute orders.

Does Rook predict which outcome will happen?

No. It shows what your positions pay under explicitly modelled outcomes. Counts of outcomes are counts, not probabilities. There are no fair values and no forecasts anywhere in the product.

How are relationships computed?

From how the contracts settle, not from price correlation. The comparison, the assumptions it rests on, and the outcomes where a hedge stops holding are all laid out inside the workstation.
