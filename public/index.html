06 / A FEW GOOD QUESTIONS

Clarity starts
right here.

What is Rook?

A trading workstation for prediction-market portfolios. It brings venue order books, contract relationships and portfolio exposure into one terminal, so a trade can be judged against the whole book rather than one line of it.

Who is it built for?

Traders and research teams running multiple prediction-market positions — especially where contracts share an underlying driver but differ in timing, threshold or settlement condition.

What can I try today?

The interactive demo carries a fictional portfolio over real Polymarket contracts on US rates. Explore exposure, scenarios, relationships, proposed trades and source evidence with private demo access.

Which parts of the terminal are product previews?

The terminal, the aggregated venue book and the opportunity scanner shown on this page are product previews built from real contracts. The demo covers the research side today: exposure, scenarios, computed relationships and settlement evidence.

How are contract relationships different from price correlation?

They are computed from how the contracts settle, not from how their prices move together. Two contracts can trade in lockstep for months and still pay differently on the one outcome that matters. The comparison, its assumptions, and the outcomes where a hedge stops holding are all laid out inside the workstation.

Why does settlement compatibility matter for aggregation and arbitrage?

Two contracts with the same headline can carry different observation windows, thresholds or resolution sources. Stacking their books averages two different questions, and an arbitrage across them can leave both legs worthless. Rook compares the rules before it groups quotes or reports an opportunity.

Is the data live? Can I place trades?

No. The demo runs on snapshot data over a fictional book. It is a research and simulation environment; it does not connect to your account and does not execute orders.

Does Rook predict which outcome will happen?

No. It shows what your positions pay under explicitly modelled outcomes. Counts of outcomes are counts, not probabilities. There are no fair values and no forecasts anywhere in the product.
